name: Capture it
line: >-
  Build the system that gathers the record accurately and continuously, as a
  by-product of the work already being done.
result: >-
  An operating system the business runs on, which produces a clean record
  because people used it — not because anyone was asked to keep notes.
---

Capture that depends on discipline fails. Anything that asks staff to log
something after the fact is abandoned inside a month, and the record it leaves
behind is worse than none because it looks complete and is not.

So the capture is the operating system. Scheduling, jobs, clients, quotes,
follow-up — the software the business actually runs on, built so that using it
normally leaves the record behind. A dispatcher assigning a crew is not doing
data entry. They are dispatching a crew, and the record is what that action
writes down.

This is the move that decides whether anything else is possible, and it is the
one most often skipped. It is also where the ownership question gets settled.
Built one way, the record accumulates in a vendor's database in the vendor's
schema and leaves when the vendor does. Built the other way it accumulates on
your side, in a shape you can query. The two cost about the same.
