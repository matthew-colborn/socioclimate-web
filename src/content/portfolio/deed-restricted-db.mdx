Every parcel around these is priced in public, continuously, by three national
portals. These are not. The price is set administratively by a housing agency,
and the pricing rule is not published anywhere.

The effect shows up in the listings. Across the San Diego listings observed so
far, list price equals original price on every one — including a unit that sat
for 317 days without a single reduction. A market where nothing is ever marked
down is not a market with patient sellers. It is a market where the number is
not being negotiated.

The stock is not small, and the agency administering it cannot see it either.
The City of San Diego held 23,440 deed-restricted units as of May 2020, with
roughly 4,200 covenants expiring between 2020 and 2040. Those figures exist
because the City paid a consultant to go and count.

Three layers, built in the least glamorous order: the rulebook first, then the
covenant index keyed to parcel, then the events. Listings and sales are the part
everyone wants to start with and the part that is worth nothing without the two
beneath it.
