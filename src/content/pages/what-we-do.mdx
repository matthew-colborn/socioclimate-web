## Why we build the capture ourselves

Most data work fails at the second move rather than the fourth. A business is
told what it ought to measure, agrees, and then finds that measuring it means
somebody has to remember to type it in. Nine months later the record has holes
in it and the project is quietly dropped.

That is why the capture is the operating system rather than a layer bolted
beside one, and why we run the revenue side instead of handing over a
recommendation. The two ends of the process are the two places these projects
die.

## What makes a record worth owning

Not every record is an asset. Five conditions decide it, and the more of them
hold, the more the record is worth building.

1. **A recurring stream nobody else sees.** Not a dataset anyone can buy.
   Records that exist because you did the work.
2. **Rules that are public but scattered.** Published by a regulator, a council
   or an association, but spread across jurisdictions and formats nobody has
   pulled together.
3. **A price set administratively rather than by open bidding.** A formula, a
   schedule, a rate card. Where buyers and sellers publish what they paid, there
   is nothing left to know.
4. **Work that is tedious rather than technically hard.** Many small
   interpretation problems rather than one large engineering problem. That is
   the shape a national vendor never gets around to.
5. **Somebody keeping the history.** Not the current state — the sequence. Every
   other condition can be met late. This one cannot be bought back at any price
   once the window has passed.

That is what the [diagnostic](#03-contact) scores, and it is usually enough to tell
you inside five questions whether this is worth doing for you.

## How it earns

**Publish existence, sell depth.** That a thing exists is usually the public
good, and giving it away costs almost nothing. The terms, the history, and the
view across everything at once are the product.

**Publish the rulebook in full.** It derives from public documents, so
withholding it captures almost no value, and publishing it makes you the
reference point for reading them. It also buys free proofreading.

**Never sell the history.** Sell findings drawn from it, access to query it,
answers out of it. The moment the series itself changes hands, the only durable
thing about the position is gone.

**Access, not extraction.** A customer who can ask questions of the record
renews. A customer handed a copy of it does not.
